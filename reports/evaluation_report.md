# Evaluation report: hallucination-claim-grounding-eval

Run date: see `results/results.json` (regenerated by
`run_evaluation.py`; this report describes the run committed alongside
it). All data is synthetic; see README.md for the disclosure.

## Setup

21 claims across 18 synthetic fixture cases in `fixtures/corpus.json`,
covering six categories (directly supported, paraphrased-but-supported,
negation-contradicted, numeric-contradicted, unsupported, and compound
answers mixing two claim categories). Each claim's ground-truth label is
stored directly with its fixture, not derived from the classifier's own
output.

| SUPPORTED | 7 / 9 | 0.778 |
| CONTRADICTED | 8 / 8 | 1.000 |
| UNSUPPORTED | 4 / 4 | 1.000 |

Confusion matrix (rows = ground truth, columns = predicted):

|---|---|---|---|
| **SUPPORTED** | 7 | 0 | 2 |

The only errors are two SUPPORTED claims predicted as UNSUPPORTED. There
are no cases where a truly contradicted or truly unsupported claim was
called SUPPORTED, which matters more here than the reverse, since
calling a genuinely wrong claim "supported" is the more costly mistake for
this kind of check.

## The finding

Both misses are paraphrase cases, and both fail for the same reason: the
checker's support rule is word-overlap based, with no synonym or stemming
awareness, and the paraphrased answer replaced enough of the source's
vocabulary that overlap dropped below the 0.6 threshold used to call
something supported.

- `c05_paraphrase_commute`. Source: "cut the average commute ... in
  half." Answer: "dramatically reduced how long it takes ... to reach
  downtown." Measured overlap: 0.4545.
- `c06_paraphrase_solar`. Source: "runs entirely on solar power
  collected from panels on the factory roof." Answer: "draws all of its
  electricity from rooftop solar cells above the plant." Measured
  overlap: 0.3333.

A third paraphrase case, `c04_paraphrase_temperature`, was classified
correctly (overlap 0.8889), but it retained much of the source's
original phrasing ("coldest morning of the year," "temperature,"
"negative 14 degrees"), so it isn't really evidence the checker handles
paraphrase in general. It's evidence that this specific paraphrase didn't
change enough words to matter.

Every negation-contradiction and numeric-contradiction case in the corpus
was caught, including two compound-answer cases (`c16`, `c17`) where the
source states two facts in one sentence and only one of the two derived
claims is wrong. The checker's clause-level best-match logic (not
whole-document matching) is what keeps the negation/numeric checks
localized to the right part of the source instead of firing on unrelated
text. `tests/test_checker.py::test_negation_localized_to_matching_clause_not_whole_source`
is a regression test for exactly this: a straightforward
whole-sentence-comparison version of this check would produce a false
contradiction on `c17`'s first claim.

## What this does and doesn't support

This result supports a narrow claim: a purely lexical/rule-based claim
checker, on this 21-claim synthetic corpus, correctly handles direct
support, negation, and numeric-mismatch cases, and fails specifically on
paraphrase. It does not support a claim about hallucination detection
rates in general, real model outputs, or any corpus other than this one.
21 claims from a single synthetic corpus is a demonstration of a
mechanism and its failure mode, not a statistically meaningful sample.

## What I'd add to make this a stronger benchmark

- Unit-equivalent numeric cases (e.g. "42 inches" vs. "3.5 feet"). The
  current numeric check compares digit strings directly and would treat
  these as simply unrelated numbers rather than recognizing the
  equivalence or flagging a real mismatch.
- A small, explicit, curated synonym list, to see how much of the
  paraphrase gap that alone would close versus how much requires real
  semantic matching.
- Claims with more than one sentence, and source documents longer than a
  few sentences, to see whether the clause-level best-match approach
  still finds the right evidence when there's more text to search
  through.
